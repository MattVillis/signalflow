2026-11-26

  • Flags no longer touch. A flag is now a little shorter than a port row, so flags on neighbouring ports stand apart without adding gaps, on a patch panel's symbol too. A flag with room to spare also keeps its whole text: one exactly 14 characters long used to lose its device name.
  • One line style per cable. A cable between two kinds of video connector, such as 12G SDI to 3G SDI, is drawn all the way in the slower end's style instead of half and half, and a DisplayPort to HDMI cable is drawn as HDMI. The legend lists the styles as they are drawn.
  • Cable labels keep the device name. A label at the end of a cable now uses the room along the cable instead of stopping at 26 characters. Where it still doesn't fit, the device name goes on a line of its own above the port, wherever the line above is free. Labels also stop at the page frame instead of running off the paper.
  • Rails and way round are two settings. In the rack view a unit has a REAR RAILS button and a REAR-FACING button, and the device editor has a tick for each: a KVM sender on a rear shelf can hang from the rear rails with its connectors facing the back of the rack. Racks you have already drawn keep what they had.
  • A unit behind another at the same U. A shallow unit on the rear rails can share a U with one on the front rails when their depths fit in the rack, as plates and patch panels already could. The front view draws it behind, the rear view in front, and OVERLAP shows only when they really don't fit.
  • Move a shelf or a group in one go. In the rack view, Shift+click adds a unit to the selection and a drag moves them all; a press on a shelf takes everything standing on it. Group ties the selected units together, so a press on one of them takes them all; Ungroup unties them.
  • UK 13 A, cloverleaf and figure of 8. UK 13A and IEC C5, C6, C7 and C8 are in the connector lists and are drawn as themselves on rack panels, so a 13 A strip no longer looks like an IEC strip.
  • A power list. Lists has a POWER tab: a row per outlet with a cable on it, what it feeds, its watts and amps, then each source's total. A strip on a UPS outlet counts with everything it feeds, so the UPS outlet carries the strip's whole load. Set the mains voltage for the amps at the top of the list.
  • PoE against the switch's budget. A POE tab weighs each switch's PoE load, from the devices cabled to its PoE ports, against its PoE budget and says what is left. The built-in NETGEAR switches carry NETGEAR's budgets; give a switch symbol of your own its budget in the device editor's PHYSICAL row, or let the AI symbol builder find it.
  • Looms. Put a cable in a loom or multicore by typing its name in the cable's LOOM field (for several cables at once too, or in the CABLES list). Lists gets a LOOMS tab: a row per loom with its cables, its ends, the cable types in it, its longest run and the cable numbers.
  • Hand-made panels. A patch panel can be narrower than the rack: give it a width in millimetres in its designer, and its place across the rack in the bar, to draw a small unit's panel to scale; two can sit side by side in one U. A position can be left EMPTY: no hole, nothing drawn.
  • Vertical power strips. Mount a symbol as a vertical strip (0U) in the PHYSICAL row and give it its length: it stands on an upright beside the rails, with its outlets drawn down it in the rear view. Drag it from the tray into a rack (it goes on the rear uprights), choose the left or right post in the bar or with ← →, and cable its outlets like any others. The AI symbol builder knows vertical strips too.
  • Card connectors where they really are. In a symbol with cards, each card connector can now be placed on its own in the panel editor, like a chassis port: click a card to pick it, click one of its connectors again to pick just that one, and drag it. Shift-click adds the card's other connectors, Spread evenly lines them up, and Back into its card undoes it. For a card that stands upright, picking it and setting a narrow CARD WIDTH stacks its connectors top to bottom in one go.
  • Notes on the Racks tab. + Note in the rack bar (or the toolbar's Note while the Racks tab is open) writes a note beside the selected rack. Drag it where it belongs, double-click it to change it, and choose whether it shows in this view only or in the front, rear and side views. Notes move with their rack and go into the SVG, PDF and DXF of the racks.
  • Arrowheads clear of the cable labels. The label at a cable's arrow end now starts past the arrowhead, however heavy the line, and so do a DAC or DMX universe tag at that end.
  • SFP modules. An SFP or QSFP port has a MODULE field in the device editor (tick several ports to set them all at once): an optic such as 10GBASE-LR or 10G BIDI 1270, or a DAC. It shows under the port on the drawing, in the cable popover (where either end can be changed), in the CABLES list (MODULES), in SWITCH PORTS (MODULE) and on the equipment list as OPTICS. Each end keeps its own value, so a BiDi pair on 1270 and 1330 needs no converter; two ends that will not link (SR against LR, 10G against 1G, a BiDi pair on one wavelength) get a note.
  • Connectors from elsewhere are kept. A connector the app does not list, from a file or another tool, shows in the port editor as it is and is no longer lost when the port is edited.
  • Trunks show their untagged VLAN. A trunk port's line on the drawing reads 11 + TAG 12,13, as the switch port list does, instead of leaving the untagged VLAN out.
  • Rear mounting at the same U. A unit dropped from the tray in the REAR view goes on the rear rails, turned so its connectors face the rear door, so it can share a U with a unit in front when their depths fit. In the SIDE view, drag a unit toward the front, mid or rear rails to hang it there (a drag there used to take it out of the rack). A place that is taken says which rails it would fit on.
  • Vent panels. A vented blank panel joins the blank, brush and fan panels in the accessories.
  • Mid rails. MID RAILS in a rack's bar gives it a third pair of rails, and how far behind the front ones they sit. The rails button then goes front, mid, rear. A unit on the mid rails only clashes with what reaches them.
  • Shelves. A SHELF accessory: drop boxes onto it and they stand on it. Drag the shelf, or move it with the arrows or its U box, and everything on it goes with it, in one undo step.
  • Long headings take their whole row. A group heading longer than its half of a symbol used to run over the port label on the other side. It now takes its row on both sides: the port opposite moves down a row and the heading stays whole. A few built-in symbols with long card names (openGear frames, dLive, DiGiCo and ETC Apex with some cards) had the same overlap and are a row taller where they did.
  • LAGs in the switch config. In Lists › SWITCH CONFIG a LAG's VLANs now go on the LAG itself (interface lag 1) and its ports join it with addport lag 1, instead of a note telling you to finish it by hand. Each LAG can be LACP or static: pick it in the LAGS row, and the drawing keeps it. On an M4500 it is interface port-channel and channel-group … mode active.
  • VLAN 1 off trunks too. The tick that takes access ports out of VLAN 1 now does the same for trunks that don't carry VLAN 1, so a trunk no longer sends VLAN 1 untagged beside its native VLAN.
  • SFP to LC is a cable. An SFP, SFP+, SFP28 or QSFP port can be cabled to an LC, SC or opticalCON port: no more "add a converter", and no CONVERTER REQUIRED in the cable list. Until the port's MODULE says what is in the cage, the list names the optic to fit (SFP+ LC OPTIC). A DAC or a copper module against fibre, or an optic against an RJ45, gets a note.
  • VLANs through patch panels. A cable on a patch panel takes the VLAN of the cable on the panel's other side, through tie lines between panels too: it is drawn in that VLAN's colour and listed with it, and a switch port with no VLAN of its own takes the VLAN of what is patched in through the panel.

2026-11-25

  • The AI symbol builder knows every connector. Building a symbol with AI (from a web search or a picture of the panel) now offers every connector SignalFlow has: RCA, TOSLINK, 3.5 mm, S/PDIF, screw and speaker terminals, CA-COM, ULTRANET, Schuko, VGA, HD-BNC and about forty more were missing from its list, so those connectors could come back blank.

2026-11-19

  • All sheets in one PDF. The Export menu has a new SHEET row: pick ALL SHEETS and Download PDF puts every sheet with something on it into one file, a page each in sheet order, with a bookmark per sheet. Print does the same in one print window. Each page keeps its own frame and title block, and the SIGNAL filter applies to every page. The SVG file is still just the sheet on screen.
  • Download PDF works without the internet. The PDF writer and its fonts now come with SignalFlow, in two files beside the app: on the site, and in the download zip. Download PDF no longer needs the font and library servers, so it works on a locked-down venue network or with no network at all. If you copy the app somewhere, keep the two signalflow-pdf files next to signalflow.html. Japanese, Chinese and Korean text still needs the internet for its font.
  • Hostname, MAC and asset number. The device editor has a row for each device's hostname, MAC address and asset number. A MAC can be typed any of the usual ways and is kept as 00:1B:44:11:3A:B7. Lists has a new DEVICES list, a row per device with its model, sheet, rack and U, IP, hostname, MAC and asset number, and the switch port list now shows the hostname at the far end. Ctrl+F finds a device by any of them.
  • Drawing title, revision and status in the title block. Each sheet can have its own drawing title (it shows the sheet's name until you type one), and the project has a status (PRELIMINARY, FOR COMMENT, FOR CONSTRUCTION, AS BUILT, or your own) and a revision. The title block's date is now the revision's date: a new revision is dated today, and you can change it. A drawing with no revision still shows the day it was printed.
  • Fixed: applying a port change to every instance of a symbol gave them all the same IP address. Each device now keeps its own.
  • The project file format, written down. A new page, mattvillis.github.io/signalflow/file-format.html, describes the .signalflow.json file key by key for anyone who writes drawings from a script or a database. It covers what each key means, what SignalFlow fills in for you, and what stays compatible from one 2.x release to the next. A JSON Schema sits next to it, the list of built-in symbols now gives every port's id, and Settings links to the page.
  • Hand-made project files open instead of failing. A few things SignalFlow never writes itself, such as a port on neither side of its symbol, a note with no text, or a sheet number out of range, used to leave a blank page. They are now repaired as the file opens, and a project file with no project name can be saved again.
  • DXF export, for Vectorworks and other CAD. The Export menu has a DXF file. It writes the sheet in millimetres at paper size, on layers that import as classes: one per signal (VIDEO, NETWORK, AUDIO …) plus DEVICES, FLAGS, TEXT, ZONES, LEGEND, TITLE_BLOCK and FRAME. Text stays editable text and line hops are real arcs. The ghost and SIGNAL filters work as they do for a PDF. On the Racks tab it writes the racks at full size. Pictures can't go in a DXF, so they are left out and the export says how many.
  • Cable type and length, and a cable order to send a supplier. Every cable can now carry its type and length. Type them straight into the cable list (Lists → Cables), or select cables on the sheet and set them in the box that appears, several at once. A new Cable order tab adds them up into what to order: each type at each length, how many and which cable numbers, with a breakout cable counted once. Fill from their connectors gives every cable without a type a first guess to edit (CAT6A for copper Ethernet, otherwise its connectors). Lengths are shown in metres or feet, chosen per project, and the CSV, PDF and printable lists carry it all. The column that used to say TYPE (direct, routed, flag) is now called DRAWN AS, which is what it always meant.
  • Cable labels. Each end of a cable now names the other end in small text, the way a flag does: its device and port, just above the cable where it leaves the port. The labels never run into another cable, a device, a flag or each other: they shorten to fit, and an end with no room for one has none. They print and export with the drawing, and can be turned off for a project in Settings → Drawing.
  • Flags no longer run over the device next to them. A flag was always as long as its text, so a symbol with a column of off-page cables on one side covered the device beside it, and that device's own flags with it. A flag now stops short of whatever is next to it on its row and shortens its text to fit: the device name goes first, then the sheet, then the port. Two flags facing each other share the gap. The full text is in the tooltip and the lists, and a flag gets it back as soon as there is room. Exports show the flags as they are on screen.
  • A1 and A0 paper. Both sit next to A4, A3 and A2 in the Export menu, for the page frame, Print and Download PDF. A file at A1 or A0 still opens in an older SignalFlow, which shows the same frame and prints it scaled down onto A2.
  • The switch config knows the NETGEAR M4300. A switch named M4300 now gets a change set headed M4300 instead of "M4250 / M4350" (its ports were already numbered 1/0/1, 1/0/2 and on, as an M4300's are), and an M4300-96X is told that it numbers its ports by slot. A switch SignalFlow doesn't recognise says so instead of claiming a model. The commands were checked against NETGEAR's M4300 CLI reference (firmware 12.0.15).

2026-11-17

  • Patch panels on the sheet. A patch panel added on the Racks tab now draws itself as a symbol on the sheet with that rack's kit (or the sheet that is open), so you can cable through it: each connector has a port at the back on the left and one at the front on the right, labelled as on the panel, with the panel's rack and U under its name. It follows the design: rename it, change its connectors, add a row or take one out, and the symbol changes with it (a cable on a connector you took out comes off, and you are told how many first). The designer's new SHEET row moves the symbol to another sheet or takes it off, and ⇄ puts the front on the left for a panel that brings signals in. Double-click the symbol to open the designer; Show on sheet in the rack bar goes the other way, and the cabled connectors show filled on the rack drawing. A trace goes straight through a connector to its other side, the call sheet lists the panel once (as a rack accessory), and the cable list says which side of it each cable is on.
  • Patch panels you made before get their symbols too. Opening a project, from a file or with Restore, now puts every patch panel that has no symbol yet on its sheet, beside its rack's kit, as a new one would be, and says how many. A panel whose symbol you delete, or take off in its designer, stays off.
  • Setting a VLAN on a switch port no longer hides the other ports. On a laptop-sized screen the device editor's port list shrank to nothing as soon as one port was changed, so the next port could only be set after applying and opening the editor again. The list now keeps a few ports in view and the window scrolls instead.
  • Rack accessories of any height, with the room found for you. Making a plate, fan panel, drawer or patch panel taller (in the bar or in the patch panel designer) used to be refused whenever something sat directly above it, which a new accessory nearly always has. It now grows down instead, or moves to the nearest place with room and says so. Made smaller again, it stays tucked under whatever is above it. An accessory dragged onto a taken row, from the tray or from another place, goes into the nearest free row instead of being refused.

2026-11-16

  • The AI symbol builder brings back the rack data too. Research a model number and the symbol now comes back with its rack size (U and width, or a desktop box), depth, weight, power in W and heat in BTU/h, taken from the maker's specifications (the page is linked above the preview), and which panel each connector is on. When it finds the maker's picture of the panel (a connections drawing, a photo of the back), it puts every connector where it really is. The preview has the PHYSICAL row to check or fix the figures, a PANEL column (front or rear) for each port, and a drawing of the front and rear panels; a connector whose panel is known but not its place is drawn dashed. Analyse a drawing instead and it reads the same off the picture: give it one with both the front and the rear (or a PDF page with both) and it places the connectors on both panels. Once added, the rack view draws the symbol with its connectors on the right panel, and their places travel with it to the shared library.
  • Send all your symbols at once, no GitHub account needed. Share your symbols now lists every symbol you made that the shared library doesn't have yet, all ticked: press Send and they go in for review in one go, without a GitHub account. Add your name or email if you'd like the reviewer to credit you or ask about one (it shows on the public review, so leave it empty to stay anonymous). A symbol that looks like one already in the library starts unticked: tick it if it really is a different device, and the reviewer is told so. Open it from Share my symbols… in the shared-library window, or a symbol's ⇡. Sending through GitHub yourself is still there for each symbol.
  • Put the connectors where they really are. In the AI symbol builder's review the front and rear panels are an editor: drag any connector (a dashed one whose place was not found, or one in the wrong spot) to where it sits, on either panel. It snaps to a grid, to the row and column of the connectors around it, and to a row's own spacing, so the next connector of a bank lands one step along (hold Alt to place it freely). Shift-click or drag a box to pick several and move them together; SPREAD EVENLY lines the picked ones up from the first to the last, and 2 ROWS puts them in odd and even rows the way a switch numbers its ports. The same editor is under PANELS in the device editor of your own symbols, so the places can be put right after the symbol is added.
  • Flags to somewhere off the drawing. Hold 4 and click a port: a box asks where its cable goes (the OB truck, the venue's patch, the next room) and, if you like, the line or input there. The cable is drawn as a flag naming that place, its tail cut like a pennant so it reads as leaving the drawing, and it is a real cable: numbered, in the cable list as EXTERNAL, found by Ctrl+F, copied and pasted with its device and deleted with it, while the place itself never turns up as kit in the equipment list or the rack tray. Places you have used are offered again. Double-click the flag to rename it, drag it to another port to move it (it turns round for an input), and if you have already started a cable, hold 4 and click anywhere to send it off the drawing.
  • Move whole racks, and arrange them in rows. On the Racks tab, drag a rack by its frame anywhere: along its row, up onto a new row to stack it over another, down into the row below, or into a gap. The others make room as you drag, so you see where it will land: four racks as a 2 × 2, three over one, whatever suits the page. The racks of a row stand on one floor, so a stacked rack sits over the one below it. With a rack selected, ↑ ↓ move it a row and ← → along one (Shift to the ends of its row), as do the arrows in the rack bar. Its devices go with it, the front, rear and side views keep the layout, and each move is one step to undo.
  • Size and power for the symbols already in your projects. Built-in symbols have always taken their size, weight, power and heat from the app, in older projects too. A shared library symbol placed on a sheet now gets its size and power from the shared library, even while a change to its ports is held back to keep your cables where they are. A symbol of your own given its size and power in one project passes them on to your other projects on the same computer when you open them. And a symbol of your own with no figures can have them looked up: Look up size and power in its device editor, Look up in the rack bar, or Look them up on the Racks tab for all of them at once. The AI engine finds them on the maker's pages in the background, with the connectors' places where it finds a picture of the panel, and you check each result and its source before using it.
  • Rack accessories: blank and brush plates, fan panels, drawers, and patch panels you design. The tray on the Racks tab has an ACCESSORIES section: drag one into a rack, onto the face you are looking at, or click one to put it in the selected rack. Select it to set its height (any number of U), a label, the face it is on, a drawer's depth or a fan panel's number of fans; ↑ ↓ move it and Delete takes it out. A patch panel opens a designer: its name, height, rows and how many across, and each connector's type (XLR male or female, etherCON, BNC, opticalCON and the rest, or an empty or blanked D hole) and label, numbered in one go. A plate covers what is behind it on its face, so a rear panel can sit behind a shallow device when the rack is deep enough; seen from the other side it is drawn faintly, a patch panel's connectors mirrored as they look from behind. The eye in the tray shows or hides every accessory, to see what is behind them or to print the racks without them.
  • Rack accessories on the call sheet. Lists → Equipment for all sheets now lists the racks' blank and brush plates, fan panels, drawers and patch panels under RACK ACCESSORIES, one row per item as you would order it (a drawer with its depth, a fan panel with its number of fans, a patch panel with its connectors counted by type), with the rack and U of each. They are in the CSV, the PDF and the printable lists too.

  • Rack elevations. A RACKS tab at the right of the sheet tabs draws every rack of the project. Add a frame (42U to start; its name, height and depth are in the bar once it is selected), then drag devices in from the tray on the right, or double-click a symbol on a sheet and give it a rack and a U in the new RACK row. FRONT, REAR and SIDE views; a device can be mounted rear-facing (the bar's REAR-FACING button, or the tick in the editor) and each view shows the panel that really faces you. Drag a device to another U, across the row or into another rack, or select it and press ↑ ↓ to move it a U (Shift for 5; it goes on past a neighbour to the next free space) and ← → along its row; the U box in the bar moves it as you change it. A taken space is refused with a message; drop a device outside every rack and it is out. A device that overlaps another or runs past the top of its rack (a custom symbol made taller, say) is outlined in red. Del takes the selection out of its rack (the symbol stays on its sheet), F fits, Ctrl+Z undoes it all.
  • Every built-in symbol knows its size, power and heat. 368 symbols researched from the manufacturers' documents: rack units, width (full, half, third, quarter, or a desktop box drawn on a shelf), depth, weight, power in W and BTU/h, and where each connector sits on the front or rear panel, so the rack view draws the real thing. Each rack's footer totals its devices, free U, watts, BTU/h and kg. A custom symbol gets the same in the new PHYSICAL row of the symbol and device editors; it is saved on the symbol and travels with the shared library.
  • Panels drawn the way they look. In the FRONT and REAR views every connector is drawn at its real size and shape: BNCs, RJ45s, etherCON, XLR, speakON and powerCON in their round housings with the two screws, HDMI, DisplayPort, USB, SFP and QSFP cages, IEC inlets and outlets, D-subs, terminal blocks and the rest. A connector with a cable in it is filled with the cable's colour, and zoomed in each one has its label printed beside it. A card's connectors stand in a column in its slot, as on the real frame. Rack units have their ears on the rails, with their slots and screws, and the rails show their holes. Every unit lines up with the ones above and below it: a full-width box sits centred between the rails, two half-width units side by side meet in the middle as in a dual rack kit, and thirds and quarters spread evenly across the same width.
  • Symbols with no rack data go in a rack too. Your own symbols, shared ones and imported ones (a UPS you drew, say) used to count as not rack-mountable and never reached the tray. They are now listed under WITH NO RACK SIZE and go in as 1U. Select one in a rack and give it its height and width in the bar: it is saved on the symbol, so every device of that symbol follows. The device editor's RACK row works for them as well.
  • Click a port in the rack view and the bar names its cable, the device at the far end and the cable number; when that device is racked too, both ports are ringed and joined. Show on sheet goes to the cable on its sheet.
  • Cable in the rack view. Click a free port, then the port at the other end, and the cable is made, on the sheet as well as in the rack, as if you had drawn it there. The two ends can be in different racks, and on the front or the rear: switch the view in between. While you choose, the ports that cable cannot go to fade. The sheet's rules apply: two inputs are refused with the reason, a port that already has a cable asks before it is replaced, and a breakout's trunk stays ready for its next lane. The cable runs from the output to the input whichever you click first. Two devices on one sheet get a direct cable (hold 3 as you click for flags); on two sheets it is an off-page reference. Unplug in the bar takes a cable out, Esc lets go of a port, and Ctrl+Z undoes either.
  • Delete in the rack view takes out the cable you picked. Click a port with a cable in it and press Delete: the cable goes, from the sheet as well, the way Unplug in the bar does it, and both devices stay in their racks. Before, Delete took the port's device out of its rack instead. With a free port waiting for its other end, Delete lets go of it; press it again to take the selected device out of its rack.
  • Export the rack elevations. With the RACKS tab open, Export writes the racks as shown (SVG, Print, Download PDF) on a page the shape of the drawing.
  • Project file version 4. Racks and each device's place ride in the file. An older SignalFlow still opens a 2.0 file and shows the drawing; it just does not know the racks (a file saved from 1.x loses them).
